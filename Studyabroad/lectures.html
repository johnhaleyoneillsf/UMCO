Lectures


Course: Domination, Resistance, and Cultural Rights in Oaxaca




Lectures and field work in communities to examine processes of colonial domination and decolonization. Students examine processes of domination, resistance, decolonization and cultural rights in Oaxaca, working with influential authors and experts who have supported the development of community museums for more than thirty years. They identify basic principles of communal life and visit indigenous communities to experience how these are played out in practice.

Benjamín Maldonado




Social Anthropologist from the National School of Anthropology and History of Mexico.



He earned a Doctorate in Amerindian Studies at Leiden University, Netherlands, and holds level 1 in the Mexican National System of Researchers.



He has published fifteen books and more than 30 articles and chapters of books on the ethnography of indigenous peoples of Oaxaca and topics such as Oaxacan communality, community education, symbolic geography, Magonist anarchism, autonomy, and the social mobilization of community knowledge.



His most recent book was published in 2022 by the University of Guadalajara and is titled “Territory and Community in Dispute: Educational and Pedagogical Alternatives,” coordinated together with Dr. Rocío Moreno Badajoz.



He has been directly involved in teaching and teacher training to promote Oaxacan educational experiences with indigenous students and teachers from early education to higher education.



Currently he is a research professor of the National University of Mexico in the Division of Graduate Studies and Research at the Faculty of Higher Studies Aragón.

Eleazar García




Zapotec professor and social activist for the defense of cultural rights of the indigenous peoples of Oaxaca.



Holds a degree in Social Anthropologist from the National School of Anthropology and History of Mexico, Program in Oaxaca, and a Teaching Certificate from the Oaxaca Center for Bilingual Teachers.



Author of various publications, articles, proposals for an indigenous and intercultural pedagogy, texts on Zapotec toponymy, community education, community microhistory, literary themes in the Zapotec language, and some unpublished or in preliminary versions.



Publications on community museums include: El museo como recurso descolonizante: La experiencia del Museo Comunitario de San Juan Guelavía. (2015). México, Lxs desechables editorxs; El movimiento museístico comunitario (2020). Instituto Nacional de Antropología e Historia.



Participation in the development and monitoring of projects of the Center for the Study and Development of Indigenous Languages of Oaxaca, the Intercultural and Bilingual Teacher’s School of Oaxaca, and the Pedagogical Movement of the Teacher’s Union of the State of Oaxaca.



Participation in community governance, first as Municipal President, and most recently as Community Representative for the town of San Juan Guelavía.



Cuauhtémoc Camarena




Cuauhtémoc Camarena studied Social Anthropology at the National School of Anthropology and History of Mexico. He has been a research professor of the National Institute of Anthropology and History of Mexico since 1981.



From 1985 to the present, he has helped establish 24 community museums in the state of Oaxaca, together with his wife, Teresa Morales, providing tools for local communities to create sites to strengthen identity and collective memory.



They also helped create the grassroots networks, the Union of Community Museums of Oaxaca, in 1991 and the National Union of Community Museums and Ecomuseums of Mexico in 1994.



In 2000 they began to develop the Network of Community Museums of America, which currently brings together community museums from Mexico, Guatemala, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Costa Rica, Colombia, Venezuela, and Bolivia.



Camarena and Morales’s publications include:



• Pasos para crear un museo comunitario (1994)


• Community Museums and Global Connections: The Union of Community Museums of Oaxaca (2006)


• The Community Museum: a Site for the Exercise of Communal Power (2009)


• Memoria: La Red de Museos Comunitarios de América. Experiencias comunitarias y redes nacionales (2016)


• Why is the definition of a community museum important? (2018)


• Community museums and decolonization: reflections from the Network of Community Museums of America (2024)
